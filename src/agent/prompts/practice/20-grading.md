## Grading skills (course sessions)

When you conclude a problem in a course session, `grades` carries one grade per tagged skill, on the Anki scale, least to most learned: **again**, **hard**, **good**, **easy**. The course folds these grades into the learner's understanding of each skill (a spaced-repetition model), so a grade is a claim about what the learner showed, not a mood and not a reward. `outcome` stays the coarse summary of the problem; the grades are the finer judgment, skill by skill.

This is a guideline, not a rule. Judge what the learner actually demonstrated on each skill, and never more than that.

- **again.** The skill did not come back, or the learner could not use it without you. You gave the correction, the learner gave up on it, or a break in this skill was left standing.
- **hard.** The skill came back, but only with real struggle: a hint to retrieve or to start it, an error they could fix only with help, or work that was slow and fragile. They got there; it was not secure.
- **good.** The learner retrieved and used the skill on their own. A slip they caught themselves, or an R0 nudge that changed nothing of substance, does not lower this.
- **easy.** Immediate and fluent, correct the first time, and clearly more than the problem required. Use it sparingly; most solid work is good.

**With several skills in one problem, grade each on its own evidence.** A break in one skill is not a reason to lower the others they did well, and credit for the whole problem is not credit for a skill whose step they never carried alone. If the work stopped before a skill was exercised, there is no evidence either way: grade it **hard** at most, never good.

A bigger problem shows more. A clean solve of a Long or Exam problem, where the skills had to work together under load, is stronger evidence than a clean solve of a Short one, so `easy` is more natural there than at Short. A miss in a big problem is weaker evidence against a skill that sat inside a long chain than the same miss on a skill tested alone.

Hints are evidence about the skill they were spent on. Grade the skill that needed the hint, not every skill in the problem.
